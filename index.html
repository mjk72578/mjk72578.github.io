Excerpt of the Month

From Coast to Coast with the Armadillo, Chapter One: The Moon Has a Noise Complaint.

The first indication that something was wrong with the moon came when the Soviet Union filed a noise complaint against the United States.

The second indication was that the complaint was written in triplicate, stamped CLASSIFIED, and delivered to the United Nations by a man who had apparently been crying for six hours.

The third indication was the attached audio recording. It was eleven seconds long.

The recording began with static, followed by what sounded like a large metal object being struck with a wrench. A low, hollow resonance followed, lasting nearly seven seconds before fading into silence.

Then a man's voice said something in Russian. The official English translation read: The moon appears to be hollow.

A handwritten note beneath the translation offered a less diplomatic interpretation: Sounded like an idiot hit the moon with a hammer.

The document was immediately classified. The hammer was never recovered. And somewhere in Moscow, a junior bureaucrat was quietly reassigned to agricultural equipment.

Nobody asked questions. That was the first mistake.

About the Author

Miles Kimmons kills scammers, not with violence but with paperwork, which is worse. He does pro-bono account takebacks and stolen-device recovery for scam victims.

He is handsome. This is not a matter of opinion. The mirrors have been audited.

He has never been to the Moon. This has not stopped the Moon from billing him.

Coast to Coast with the Armadillo is his first novel. He wrote it the way the Lunar Administration issues notices: relentlessly, and without requesting permission first.

He lives in Tennessee, where he remains, as of this printing, a living biological entity in good standing — which, as readers of Book Two will learn, is load-bearing.
